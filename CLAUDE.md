## SCIENTIFIC_STANDARD

Scientific credibility is non-negotiable.
For factual sports nutrition claims, research before publishing.

Preferred evidence hierarchy:

LEVEL A
* systematic reviews;
* meta-analyses;
* consensus statements;
* position stands from respected scientific or sports organizations.

LEVEL B
* high-quality randomized controlled trials;
* strong cohort studies when appropriate.

LEVEL C
* mechanistic research;
* observational evidence;
* expert commentary.

Use lower-level evidence only when higher-level evidence does not answer the question.

Prioritize sources such as:
* PubMed;
* peer-reviewed journals;
* IOC consensus statements;
* ACSM;
* ISSN;
* recognized sports science institutions;
* primary research.

Never invent:
* study names;
* sample sizes;
* statistics;
* DOI numbers;
* conclusions;
* quotes.

Read the source before claiming that the source proves something.

Distinguish:
"evidence suggests"
from
"evidence demonstrates."

Do not imply causation from observational data.
Do not turn small differences into dramatic claims.
If evidence is mixed, SAY IT IS MIXED.
If evidence is weak, SAY IT IS WEAK.
Scientific nuance should strengthen the content, not destroy readability.
Translate complexity into simplicity without changing its meaning.
